## Capstone Handoff Pitch

The final demonstration is not a tour of everything you did in CTS-285. It is a concise handoff: what the next course is receiving, what decisions are already supported, and what it must not assume is settled.

How this is graded

This pitch is required and formative. It carries no separate points; the course does not grade presentation performance.

### Show five things

1. **Who arrives:** pod provenance and the working agreement.
2. **What is being handed forward:** product status, backlog, design evidence, and brief.
3. **What CTS-285 proved:** one example of evidence that survived analysis → design → implementation → testing/review.
4. **What could derail the next stage:** the highest-value risk/dependency.
5. **What remains unknown:** one decision or question the capstone should not silently answer for itself.

### Use the package as evidence

Open the relevant artifacts while you speak. A handoff claim should point to something the receiving team can inspect after the pitch is over.

**Avoid:** chronology by module, long feature demos, reading every risk, or spending the pitch proving how much work the class completed. The next course needs orientation and decision evidence, not a semester recap.

### Individual accountability still applies

Each student should speak to the handoff component they owned or validated and be able to point to their own evidence. The pitch is shared context, not a shared grade.

**After the pitch:** make any final clarity repairs surfaced by questions, then leave the handoff package in its final repository location.
