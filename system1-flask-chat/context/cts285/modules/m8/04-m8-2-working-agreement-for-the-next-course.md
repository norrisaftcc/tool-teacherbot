## Working Agreement for the Next Course

Your pod already learned to work together under course-set sprint rules. The capstone handoff needs a working agreement that makes the team's operating expectations visible before a longer project begins.

### Harden; do not start over

Begin with the agreement and operating practices your pod used in M6 and M7. Keep what worked, revise what your retrospectives exposed, and preserve course-set rules that still apply. Do not invent a new process simply because the file is being prepared for another course.

### The agreement must answer

Area | What another course needs to know

**Communication** | primary channel, expected response window, and how time-sensitive issues are marked;

**Coordination** | when the team expects to meet/synchronize and how asynchronous work is handed off;

**Work visibility** | where commitments, pull requests, review status, and unresolved blockers are visible;

**Missed commitments** | what happens when planned work will not land and how scope is reduced rather than hidden;

**Escalation** | the course-set escalation threshold/process already used by the pod, unless the receiving course replaces it;

**Quality** | how the team expects tests, review, and the Definition of Done to function as shared evidence.

### Use retrospectives as evidence

At least one change in the hardened agreement should be traceable to something the pod learned in the M6 or M7 retrospective. A revised agreement should explain why the rule changed rather than silently replacing history.

**Suggested path:** `docs/capstone/working-agreement.md`. Keep the M6/M7 evidence intact; this is the hardened capstone-facing version.

### Pod provenance belongs beside the agreement

The handoff must say whether the pod arrives intact, reduced, dissolved, as an onboarding hire, or solo by exception. That line describes the handoff condition so the next course can route support. It is not a judgment of the student.
