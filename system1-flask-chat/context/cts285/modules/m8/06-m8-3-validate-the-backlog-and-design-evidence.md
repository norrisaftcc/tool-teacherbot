## Validate the Backlog and Design Evidence

The capstone does not need every draft you created. It needs a current backlog and design evidence that still agree with the decisions the project actually made.

### Backlog validation

Review the backlog against the later course record. A handoff-ready backlog should make clear:

- which stories/criteria still represent intended product behavior;
- which items were delivered, deferred, changed, or contradicted by later evidence;
- which source conflicts remain visible rather than silently edited away;
- which priorities are still meaningful for the receiving course.

**Known example:** if your backlog still carries the inherited three-attempt criterion while the authoritative DataMan manual says two, do not erase the history. Harden the handoff by recording the conflict and the later ruling/evidence trail.

### Design evidence validation

M4 already taught wireframes/design evidence. Do not repeat that instruction here. Check whether the existing design still communicates the product the capstone is inheriting.

Check | Handoff question

**Coverage** | Do the core views/states needed by the current backlog appear?

**Terminology** | Do labels match the language the project now uses?

**Traceability** | Can a reader connect a wireframe/state to backlog or requirement evidence?

**Currency** | Did later sprint decisions make any part misleading?

**Failure/state visibility** | Does the design show at least one meaningful alternate/error state where the receiving team needs it?

### Revision rule

Revise only what makes the handoff more accurate. Preserve the original evidence trail and record why a handoff-facing artifact changed.

### What the package must make clear

A CSC-289 reader can understand what the product intends, what evidence supports it, what changed during CTS-285, and what remains unresolved without opening every module folder.
