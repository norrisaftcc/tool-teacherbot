## Handoff Readiness Check

Run the `module-08/student-facing/m8-exit-checklist.md` check from the perspective of someone entering CSC-289 with no access to your memory of the semester. A package is ready when a new reader can locate the evidence, understand its status, and see the unknowns without asking you to reconstruct the history.

How this is graded

This check does not create a second grade for the same package. Use it to repair handoff clarity before the final pitch.

### A receiving student should be able to answer

1. Who is arriving in the pod, and what is the provenance/status?
2. What working agreement governs the handoff into the next course?
3. What capstone direction is being handed forward, and which product decisions are deliberately still open?
4. Where is the validated backlog and what known conflicts or changes does it carry?
5. Where are the wireframes/design evidence and what do they represent now?
6. Where is the design brief and the required instructor-critique record? If external feedback exists, where is it recorded?
7. What are the highest-value seeded risks and open dependencies?
8. What is explicitly unknown or waiting on a decision?
9. What evidence from CTS-285 should the capstone preserve rather than recreate?

### Check the links, not just the words

Open every path/link from the handoff manifest. A perfect description pointing to a stale or inaccessible file is not a ready handoff.

**Repair rule:** if the answer exists but is hard to find, fix the manifest or context. Do not copy the entire artifact into a new document just to shorten the path.

### Leave unknowns visible

A readiness check can pass while carrying an explicitly unresolved dependency. The failure is not “unknown exists.” The failure is “unknown is hidden, ownerless, or indistinguishable from completed work.”

**Next:** the Capstone Handoff Pitch demonstrates the package as a decision-and-evidence handoff, not as a retrospective tour of the entire course.
