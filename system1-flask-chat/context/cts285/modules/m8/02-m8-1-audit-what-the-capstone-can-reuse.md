## Audit What the Capstone Can Reuse

A handoff is not an archive dump. The next course needs evidence it can understand, trust, and act on. Your first job is to decide what is already ready, what needs context, what is missing, and what is blocked by an unresolved decision.

### Use four actions

Action | Use it when

**Keep** | The artifact is current, traceable, understandable outside the module that created it, and ready to hand forward.

**Harden** | The artifact is useful but needs revision, context, cleanup, or a capstone-specific decision.

**Create** | The handoff requires evidence CTS-285 has not produced yet.

**Resolve** | The artifact cannot be finalized until a decision, dependency, or source question is settled.

### Audit evidence, not file names

A file can exist and still be unready. Ask whether a reader in the next course can answer these questions without reconstructing your semester:

- What decision or behavior does this artifact describe?
- What source or earlier evidence supports it?
- Is it still current after two sprints of change?
- What remains unresolved?
- Who owns the next action, if one exists?

Example

Your M3 backlog may be valuable and traceable, but if Sprint 1 and Sprint 2 changed priorities or exposed a source conflict, it may need to be **hardened** before handoff rather than copied unchanged.

### Do not silently repair history

If a prior artifact contains a known contradiction, preserve the record and add the correction or decision trail. A handoff that looks cleaner because the evidence trail was erased is less trustworthy, not more.
