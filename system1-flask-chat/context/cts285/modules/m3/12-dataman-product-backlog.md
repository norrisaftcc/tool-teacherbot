## DataMan Product Backlog

Turn your validated DataMan requirements into a traceable, testable, prioritized product backlog that another team member could actually use.

**This assignment is worth 20 points.**

### Start with the template

**Download the DataMan Product Backlog template**

Analyst rule

**Your backlog should show the full decision chain.**

**Validated Requirement → User Story → Acceptance Criteria → Effort / Dependency → Priority → Rationale**

### Required backlog fields

- **Story ID**
- **Requirement ID**
- **User Story**
- **Acceptance Criteria**
- **Relative Effort**
- **Dependency / Constraint**
- **Priority**
- **Priority Rationale**

### Format example

This example shows structure only. Replace the placeholders with your own validated DataMan product work.

Story ID | Requirement ID | User Story | Acceptance Criteria | Effort | Dependency / Constraint | Priority | Rationale

US-01 | FR-__ | As a... | Observable condition(s) | Smaller / Medium / Larger | Dependency, blocker, constraint, or none | Priority | Why this priority is defensible

### Final readiness check

- Every story traces to a validated requirement.
- Each story makes the user and value clear.
- Acceptance criteria are observable and testable.
- Large or uncertain work is identified honestly.
- Dependencies and constraints are visible.
- Priorities reflect relevant factors such as value, effort, dependency, risk, uncertainty, and stakeholder need.
- Your revisions reflect what you learned from backlog triage and constrained release planning.

Caution

**Do not build the solution yet.** Module 3 is about organizing and defending product work. Your job here is to produce backlog work that is ready to move into design and implementation planning—not to begin building the Flask solution.
