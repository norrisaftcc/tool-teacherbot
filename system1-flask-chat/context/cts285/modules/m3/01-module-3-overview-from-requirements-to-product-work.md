## From Requirements to Product Work

In Module 2, you used evidence to define what a modern DataMan must do. In Module 3, you will turn those validated requirements into product work a development team can understand, test, size, prioritize, and defend.

Analyst rule

**The project does not reset here.** Your Module 2 **DataMan Requirements Register** becomes the starting point for this module. You will not invent a new set of needs. You will use the requirements you already justified to create user stories, acceptance criteria, backlog decisions, and a prioritized product backlog.

Analyst rule

The backlog work in this module is also **project-management practice**: you will make scope, priority, dependency, effort/capacity, and risk decisions about what work should move forward and why.

### Module learning outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

**MLO 3.1 → CLO 2, CLO 3**

#### Develop actionable product work

Transform validated requirements into user-centered stories with clear, testable acceptance criteria and visible traceability to the underlying requirement.

**MLO 3.2 → CLO 3**

#### Analyze backlog readiness

Evaluate product work for appropriate scope, dependencies, relative effort, uncertainty, and readiness for team consideration.

**MLO 3.3 → CLO 3, CLO 5**

#### Prioritize and defend product decisions

Use value, dependencies, relative effort/capacity, risk, and stakeholder needs to prioritize product work and justify constrained backlog decisions.

Analyst rule

**Your role changes in this module.** You are still using systems-analysis evidence, but now you also need to think like a **Product Owner and product team member**. That means asking different questions:

- Who needs this capability, and why does it matter?
- What would count as acceptable behavior?
- Is this item small and clear enough for a team to work with?
- Does another item have to happen first?
- What creates the most value under limited capacity?
- What should wait, and how can that decision be defended?

### What you will do

1. Learn how validated requirements become useful user stories.
2. Practice writing acceptance criteria that make product behavior observable and testable.
3. Analyze scope, dependencies, relative effort, value, risk, and priority.
4. Make constrained Product Owner decisions in a sprint-planning simulation.
5. Review and revise your own DataMan backlog decisions using what you learned.
6. Build a professional, traceable DataMan product backlog in GitHub.

**What carries forward from Module 2?**

You will use your existing `docs/requirements.md` file as source material. The evidence, requirement IDs, open questions, and distinctions you established there matter here.

If a requirement is unresolved or unsupported, it should not quietly become committed product work. Traceability matters because the team needs to know *why* a story exists.

**What will you add to GitHub?**

This module adds three pieces of persistent evidence:

- `docs/decisions/m3-backlog-triage-record.md` — evidence from the backlog triage decision.
- `docs/decisions/m3-product-owner-decision-record.md` — evidence from the Product Owner Sprint Simulation.
- `docs/product-backlog.md` — your evaluated DataMan product backlog.

These are not disposable assignments. They will become input to later design and implementation work.

Analyst rule

**Keep one principle in view.** **A backlog is not a wish list.** It is a set of product decisions. Good backlog items remain connected to evidence, describe meaningful user value, define observable success, and can be prioritized under real constraints.

**Next:** You will begin by learning how a validated requirement becomes a useful user story without losing the need and evidence behind it.
