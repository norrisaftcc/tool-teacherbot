## M3.5 — Product Owner Sprint Simulation

You have practiced evaluating individual backlog items. Now make a release-level product decision when capacity is limited and new information changes what the team can responsibly move forward.

### Your role

Act as the Product Owner for **StudyTrack**, a student study-planning application. You have more valuable work than the team can complete. Your job is to choose a defensible release slice, explain what waits, and revise when a real constraint changes the plan.

Caution

**Use the information you are given.** Treat the backlog details in the simulation as established decision inputs. Use the stated value, effort, dependencies, readiness, risk, and release constraints to make your choices. Do not invent missing facts or stop to redesign the requirements.

### What you will do

1. Review eight candidate backlog items.
2. Build an initial release slice within **13 effort points**.
3. Defend what you selected and what you intentionally deferred.
4. Respond to a complication that reduces capacity and changes release risk.
5. Revise your plan within the new constraint.
6. Generate your Product Owner Decision Record.

[Open Product Owner Sprint Simulation](https://amlw05.github.io/cts-285-course-simulations/m3/product-owner-sprint/)

Your artifact

**Before you close the simulation.** Generate the **M3 Product Owner Decision Record** and copy it into your CTS-285 GitHub repository at `docs/decisions/m3-product-owner-decision-record.md`. The record is the graded evidence from this simulation and will also support your later DataMan backlog review.

### What counts

There is no reward for selecting the largest number of features. Strong Product Owner reasoning uses the available evidence and constraints—value, dependencies, effort, risk, readiness, accessibility, stakeholder need, and capacity—to explain why a release plan is defensible.
