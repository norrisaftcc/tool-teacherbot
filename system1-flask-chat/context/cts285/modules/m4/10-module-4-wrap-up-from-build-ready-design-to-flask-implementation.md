## Module 4 Wrap-Up — From Build-Ready Design to Flask Implementation

You began Module 4 with a product backlog and a question: **What still needs to be understood before this work is safe and clear enough to build?**

You did not answer that question by creating every possible design artifact. You practiced choosing evidence for a reason, tested design decisions in controlled situations, investigated your own product work, and then used a small working prototype to see whether the design held up when it had to run.

**The key shift:** design evidence is useful when it helps you answer a real implementation question, exposes a consequence, or gives the team enough evidence to make the next decision.

### What you should carry forward

Your Module 5 work should begin from the evidence you already created, not from a blank page.

- the product slice you chose to move forward;
- the requirements, user stories, and acceptance criteria that justify it;
- your investigation question and the design evidence you used to answer it;
- the consequences or revisions you discovered in the controlled labs and Design Studio;
- the business logic that already works in your text-only prototype;
- the state or data responsibilities that are still stubbed or unresolved;
- the acceptance criteria that the Flask implementation will still need to satisfy.

### Your prototype is a bridge, not a throwaway

Module 4 prototype layer | What happens in Module 5

**Text interface** | The interface context changes as you begin working with Flask request/response behavior.

**Business logic** | Reusable rules should stay separate enough to carry forward instead of being rewritten just because the interface changed.

**Stubbed data layer** | The data boundary remains visible so later implementation changes can happen without hiding responsibility inside the interface.

### Before you leave Module 4

**Can I explain why this product slice is ready to move forward?**

You should be able to point to requirements, backlog evidence, design evidence, and prototype behavior rather than relying on preference or momentum.

**Do I know what changed because of the evidence?**

A useful investigation may confirm your first idea, but it may also cause you to revise, split, defer, or clarify the work. Be able to name that change.

**Do I know what remains unresolved?**

Build-ready does not mean every future question is answered. It means the next implementation step is justified and the remaining uncertainty is visible.

**Next: Module 5 — First Flask Implementation.** You will change the implementation context without restarting the project. The goal is to turn the build-ready product work and reusable logic from Module 4 into a small working Flask system while preserving the evidence that justified it.

**Nothing new is submitted on this page.** Make sure your Module 4 work is complete, then continue to Module 5 when it opens.
