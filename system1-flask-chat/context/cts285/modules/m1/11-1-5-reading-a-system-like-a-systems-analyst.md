## M1.5 — Reading a System Like a Systems Analyst

Systems analysts don't begin by solving problems. They begin by understanding the system that already exists.

### Why this matters

When analysts receive documentation for an unfamiliar system, they don't immediately think about programming, redesigning, or adding features.

Instead, they carefully examine the available information to understand:

- What the system does
- Who uses it
- What problem it solves
- How information moves through the system
- What questions still need to be answered

This lesson introduces the reading strategy you'll use throughout the course.

### Read with purpose

As you read system documentation, avoid reading like a user. Read like an analyst.

Rather than trying to learn how to operate the system, look for evidence that helps you understand how the system works.

### Questions analysts ask

Look For… | Ask Yourself…

**Purpose** | Why does this system exist?

**Users** | Who interacts with the system?

**Inputs** | What information enters the system?

**Processing** | What happens to the information?

**Outputs** | What information does the system return?

**Feedback** | How does the system respond to the user?

**Evidence** | Where did I find this information?

**Unknowns** | What information is still missing?

### The four-step reading process

**Observe**

→

**Identify**

→

**Verify**

→

**Document**

#### Step 1 — observe

Read the documentation carefully without jumping to conclusions.

#### Step 2 — identify

Locate evidence about the users, purpose, inputs, outputs, and processes.

#### Step 3 — verify

Support every conclusion with evidence found in the documentation.

#### Step 4 — document

Record only what you know and clearly identify what cannot yet be determined.

### Observation vs. assumption

One of the most important habits of a systems analyst is separating **observations** from **assumptions**.

An observation is supported by evidence. An assumption is a conclusion made without enough evidence.

Analyst rule

If you cannot point to evidence that supports a conclusion, do not record it as a fact. Instead, identify it as an assumption or note that additional information is needed.

### Practice identifying observations and assumptions

Read each statement before revealing the answer.

****A coffee shop has a drive-through window.****

**Observation**

If you can see the drive-through window, the statement is supported by evidence.

****The coffee shop uses Square to process payments.****

**Assumption**

You would need additional evidence before making this conclusion.

****A grocery store has self-checkout kiosks.****

**Observation**

If you can clearly observe the kiosks, this statement is supported by evidence.

****Every customer prefers using self-checkout.****

**Assumption**

Customer preferences cannot be determined simply by observing the system.

### Your analyst checklist

As you read documentation throughout this course, use the same checklist every time.

- I identified the purpose of the system.
- I identified the primary users.
- I identified the major inputs.
- I identified the major outputs.
- I identified how the system processes information.
- I identified how the system provides feedback.
- Every conclusion is supported by evidence.
- I separated observations from assumptions.
