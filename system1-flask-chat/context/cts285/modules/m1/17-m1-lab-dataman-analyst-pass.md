## Module 1 Assignment — Analyst’s Case File: Number Guesser

### Case-file structure

Create one document named `m1-number-guesser-case-file` in Word, Markdown, or PDF format. Use these nine section headings:

1. Purpose
2. Users and roles
3. Inputs
4. Processing
5. Outputs
6. Feedback
7. Observations vs. assumptions
8. Unknowns
9. Cross-register note

### What you are doing

You have practiced reading system documentation as an analyst. In 1.5 you learned the four-step process. In 1.6 we worked an example together. In the two Guided Analysis quizzes you applied that process to features we examined side by side.

Now you do it alone, on a feature we never covered.

Your assignment is to analyze **Number Guesser** and produce an analyst’s case file. Number Guesser appears in both halves of the DataMan manual, and neither half tells you everything. Nobody has walked you through it. That is the point.

### Why this feature

Every other DataMan feature has been either demonstrated in a lesson or drilled in a quiz. Number Guesser has not. What you turn in will show what you can do with an unfamiliar system and a document that does not fully cooperate — which is the actual job.

### What to submit

One completed case file using the nine-section structure above. Submit as .docx, .md, or .pdf.

### Requirements

**Use both registers.** The manual documents Number Guesser twice: once in Part I as a story, once in Part II as operating notes. A case file built from only one will be missing findings, and the Cross-Register Note in Section 9 will be impossible to complete honestly.

**Quote your evidence.** Every finding needs an exact phrase from the manual and the name of the section it came from. Paraphrase is not evidence. A finding without a quote does not count, however correct it happens to be.

**Cite sections by name, not page number.** The manual carries two numbering systems that disagree with each other. Section headings are stable. Write “Number Guesser (Story),” not “page 7.”

**Fill in Section 8.** An empty Unknowns section means you either did not look hard enough or did not want to admit a gap. There are real unknowns about Number Guesser. Find them, name them, and say where you looked.

**Do not redesign anything.** You are not being asked what Number Guesser should do, how it could be improved, or what a modern version would look like. You are being asked what the documentation supports. Improvement ideas belong in a later module.

How this is graded

Criterion | Points | What earns full marks

Evidence discipline | 30 | Every finding carries an exact quote and a named section. No paraphrase presented as evidence.

Coverage of the analyst categories | 20 | Purpose, users and roles, inputs, processing, outputs, and feedback are each addressed with support.

Roles distinguished | 10 | Where more than one person interacts with the feature, the case file says what each one does rather than listing them as one undifferentiated group.

Observations separated from assumptions | 15 | Section 7 shows real self-correction — statements the writer was tempted to assert but could not support, with what evidence would settle each.

Unknowns identified | 15 | Section 8 names genuine gaps, states where the writer looked, and does not pad with things the manual actually answers.

Cross-register note | 10 | Section 9 identifies a real difference between the two halves and explains why it matters to an analyst.

**Total** | **100** |

### Common ways this goes wrong

**Recording the manual’s claims about benefits as system behavior.** Part II says Number Guesser helps teach estimation and averaging. That is a claim about educational value, not a description of what the system does. It belongs in Purpose with careful wording, not in Processing.

**Filling Unknowns with things the manual answers.** If you write “the manual does not say how the hint works,” check again — it does. Weak unknowns are worse than few unknowns.

**Treating a silence in one register as a silence in the manual.** Before you record something as undetermined, confirm that *both* Part I and Part II are silent on it.

**Listing users without roles.** “Children and families use it” is thinner than it looks. Who picks the secret number? Who guesses? Who is present but not interacting? Some of those answers are in the manual and some are not, and the difference is worth stating.
