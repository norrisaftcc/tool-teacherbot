## M1.6 — Guided Document Analysis: The Story of DataMan

Reading documentation is one of the most important skills a systems analyst develops. In this lesson, you'll analyze a real product using the same approach professional analysts use when learning an unfamiliar system.

### Required course resource

Open the published **The DataMan Manual** page in **Module 1 → Reference Resources**. Keep that page open while you work through this activity.

The manual is the governing product source for this analysis. Use its section headings when you cite evidence.

### Purpose of this activity

Rather than reading documentation as a user, you'll learn to read it as a systems analyst.

Your objective is not to redesign the system or identify improvements.

Instead, you'll learn how to identify evidence, support conclusions, and distinguish observations from assumptions.

### What you'll practice

- Reading documentation carefully
- Finding evidence that supports conclusions
- Identifying system purpose
- Recognizing users
- Separating observations from assumptions

### Step 1 — Read the documentation

Open The DataMan Manual and locate the section titled **“The Story of DataMan.”**

Read the section from beginning to end **without taking notes.**

Your first reading is simply to become familiar with the product.

Analyst rule

Professional systems analysts rarely understand a system after a single reading. The first reading builds familiarity. The second reading is used to collect evidence.

### Step 2 — Read like a systems analyst

Read **“The Story of DataMan”** in the manual a second time.

This time, don't read it as someone learning to use the product. Read it as someone trying to understand how the system works.

As you read, think about the questions below. Do not worry about writing answers yet. Simply identify evidence within the documentation.

Look for… | Ask yourself…

**Purpose** | Why does this product exist?

**Users** | Who appears to use the product?

**Evidence** | Which words or phrases support your conclusions?

**Unknowns** | What information cannot yet be determined?

### Modeling the analysis

Let's work through one example together.

#### Question

**What is the primary purpose of DataMan?**

Rather than searching for a sentence that says, “The purpose of this system is…”, an analyst combines evidence from several parts of the documentation.

****Compare your thinking****

After reading the introduction, an analyst notices repeated references to learning mathematics, practicing skills, and receiving immediate feedback.

Those observations support the conclusion that the product exists to help users practice mathematics through interactive learning activities.

Notice that the conclusion is based on evidence from the documentation rather than personal opinion.

#### Question

**Which people appear to interact with this system?**

Read the same section again and look only for references to people.

****Compare your thinking****

An analyst identifies students, teachers, parents, and independent learners because these groups are referenced throughout the documentation.

No assumptions are made about additional users because the documentation does not provide evidence for them.
