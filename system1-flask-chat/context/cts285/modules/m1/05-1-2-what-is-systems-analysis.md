## M1.2 — Systems Analysis

Analysis is investigation. Your job is to understand what is happening, who is affected, what evidence supports the problem, and what the future system actually needs to accomplish.

Caution

**A complaint, symptom, or requested feature is not automatically the underlying need.** Analysis separates what someone says from what the evidence actually supports.

### What analysts investigate

Question | Evidence to look for

**What happens now?** | Current workflow, existing behavior, records, observations, and system outputs.

**Who is affected?** | Users, stakeholders, roles, responsibilities, and differing perspectives.

**What is the problem or opportunity?** | Failures, delays, contradictions, unmet needs, constraints, and business goals.

**What should the future system accomplish?** | Validated needs translated into requirements that can later be checked.

### Evidence before conclusion

Analysts rarely get one perfect source. A manual may describe intended behavior. A stakeholder may describe what they need. An observation may show what actually happens. Those sources can agree, add context, or contradict one another.

Analyst rule

**Do not resolve conflicting evidence by choosing the version you prefer.** Record the conflict, identify which source is authoritative when that is known, and keep uncertainty visible when it is not.

### From observation to requirement

The core reasoning chain for this course begins here:

**Evidence**
What can we support?

→

**Need**
What problem must be addressed?

→

**Requirement**
What must the future system do or satisfy?

### A short analyst test

****“Users keep making mistakes, so add a confirmation screen.” What is analysis responsible for?****

First determine where the mistakes occur, why they occur, who is affected, and what evidence supports the problem. A confirmation screen may become a design option later, but it is not yet the need.

****Two sources disagree about a rule. What should the analyst do?****

Preserve the disagreement, check which source has authority, and avoid converting uncertainty into a confirmed requirement until the evidence supports it.

Outcome

Use evidence from users, current behavior, and project sources to distinguish symptoms, proposed solutions, unresolved questions, and defensible system needs.
