CTS-285 · Module 6

## Product: Sprint 1

You already have a working Flask application, tested product behavior, acceptance criteria, and a Git history that shows how your implementation changed. In this module, that work becomes the starting point for a sprint: you will grow the system with other people while keeping your own evidence reviewable and attributable.

### Driving question

**How do you add to a working system and still be able to prove it works?**

Analyst rule

Sprint scope, capacity, role ownership, review, and Definition of Done are forms of **iterative project management**. You are managing a small increment of a real product rather than completing an isolated team exercise.

### What changes in Module 6

You are not restarting DataMan, rewriting your backlog, or beginning a separate team project. The same product evidence continues forward. What changes is the way you work with it.

Carry forward

Your Flask application, product backlog, acceptance criteria, design evidence, implementation findings, unresolved questions, and Git history.

Add in Sprint 1

Executable tests, an explicit Definition of Done, evidence-based code review, sprint commitments, and individually attributable role evidence.

### Module learning outcomes

MLO 6.1 → CLO 4 — Turn Acceptance Criteria into Executable Tests

Convert an existing acceptance criterion into an executable test that can demonstrate failure before the required behavior exists and success after the behavior is correctly implemented.

MLO 6.2 → CLO 4, CLO 5 — Review an Implementation Against Evidence

Evaluate an implementation against source evidence, acceptance criteria, and observed behavior; distinguish coding defects, test defects, design differences, and source ambiguity using evidence rather than preference.

MLO 6.3 → CLO 4, CLO 5 — Plan, Deliver, and Evaluate a Sprint Increment

Plan a scoped sprint increment, contribute individually attributable work toward it, evaluate the result against an explicit Definition of Done, and use review evidence to inform the next sprint.

### How the work will progress

New development practices are taught and practiced before they are evaluated. You will move through the module in this order:

1. **Establish the pod and role responsibilities.**
2. **Learn how an acceptance criterion becomes an executable test.**
3. **Practice testing in a controlled example before your own work is evaluated.**
4. **Learn how a Definition of Done creates a standard that can actually pass or fail.**
5. **Commit to a scoped Sprint 1 increment.**
6. **Learn how to review another implementation against evidence.**
7. **Practice code review before graded review evidence is required.**
8. **Deliver, review, and evaluate the sprint work.**

### Your pod and your grade are not the same thing

You will work in a delivery pod, but there is **no group grade**. Shared work gives you a real development context. Your evaluated evidence remains your own.

Sprint 1 roles

- **Product Owner:** owns what the sprint commits to and what it refuses.
- **Developer:** owns a slice of the increment and the tests that cover that work.
- **Quality Lead:** owns the standard, review coordination, and Definition of Done evidence.

**Every student still performs every core practice.** Your role determines what you own and are evaluated on at greater depth; it does not excuse you from testing, review, or contributing to the increment.

### What will count as evidence

Module 6 uses three individually attributable evidence types:

- **Sprint 1 Contribution** — your own implementation, test, commit, and related contribution evidence;
- **Code Review Given** — your own evidence-based review of another implementation or supplied review target;
- **Role Record** — evidence that you performed the responsibilities of your assigned Product Owner, Developer, or Quality Lead role.

Your repository remains the technical source of truth. Canvas will capture the academic submission or link needed for evaluation rather than requiring duplicate code uploads.

How this is graded

Your grade will not depend on whether a teammate completes their work. When a pod loses capacity, scope changes rather than silently transferring another student's graded responsibility to you.

### Where this is going

Sprint 1 is the first full cycle. Module 7 does not start over. Roles rotate, scaffolding is reduced, and the working system must survive another round of change.

**Module 6:** learn to grow, test, review, and evaluate a working system in a pod.
**Module 7:** do it again in a different role, with less scaffolding and greater ownership.
