## Sprint 1 Planning — Commit to One Increment

Sprint planning is where the pod decides what it can honestly finish inside the timebox. The goal is not to promise the largest feature. It is to make one scoped commitment that can be tested, reviewed, and evaluated against the Definition of Done.

Outcome

**MLO 6.3 → CLO 4, CLO 5:** plan a bounded sprint increment, preserve individual ownership, and make the later review capable of comparing commitment with result.

### Start from evidence already in the project

Use your carried-forward backlog, acceptance criteria, implementation findings, and unresolved questions. Do not create a new backlog for Sprint 1.

Default scope is always available

If your pod has not agreed a scope, use the course-supplied attempts-remaining slice. It is intentionally complete and small enough to support testing, review, and Definition-of-Done evidence. Using it does not lower a grade.

### The planning decision

The Product Owner records the pod's scope decision in `docs/decisions/m6-sprint-scope-decision.md`. The record should be short enough that someone can compare it to the final increment without reconstructing the meeting.

Record | What it must answer

**Increment** | What observable product change is the pod committing to?

**Included criteria** | Which acceptance criteria are inside this sprint?

**Explicitly out** | What tempting work is being refused for now?

**Evidence** | What backlog, requirement, source, or prior finding supports the choice?

**Ownership** | What slice or responsibility belongs to each student?

**Done test** | Which Definition-of-Done checks are most likely to challenge this increment?

### Each role leaves a visible commitment

- **Product Owner:** owns the scope record and the decision to include or refuse work.
- **Developer:** identifies the slice they will implement and the tests that must cover it.
- **Quality Lead:** confirms the Definition of Done is in place before the work is judged and identifies the review path.

Those responsibilities do not replace the common practices. Every student still contributes, tests, and reviews.

### Use capacity to shrink scope, not quality

If the pod has less capacity than expected, reduce the increment. Do not delete testing, review, or Definition-of-Done checks to make a larger scope appear complete.

### Before you build

The sprint should now have one bounded increment, named acceptance criteria, individual ownership, and a Definition of Done already in place. Next, you will learn how to read someone else's implementation against evidence before review becomes graded work.
