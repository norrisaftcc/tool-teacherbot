CTS-285 · Module 6 Wrap-Up

## From Sprint 1 to Sprint 2

Sprint 1 changed the way you prove work. A requirement became a test. A test became change evidence. A Definition of Done became a standard that could expose a shortfall. A review became a source-grounded argument rather than a preference.

### What carries forward

- your working Flask application and Sprint 1 increment;
- the executable tests protecting behavior you now depend on;
- `docs/definition-of-done.md` and its current result;
- the Sprint 1 scope decision and retrospective;
- review findings, including unresolved source or design questions;
- the pod itself.

### What changes in Module 7

**Your role rotates once.** You will hold a different responsibility at depth while still performing every core practice. Scaffolding is reduced. Sprint 2 asks the same evidence loop to survive a larger technical change: persistence replaces the in-memory store when the technical build is ready for that swap.

Sprint 1

Learn the testing, review, and done-standard practices with substantial structure.

Sprint 2

Repeat the cycle in a new role with less scaffolding and more change pressure.

### Notice the process decisions you did not make

The course set the sprint length, ceremony sequence, Definition-of-Done starting point, Git workflow, escalation rule, pod formation, and rotation point. Those choices were made for you so you could first learn to work inside the process.

### What the capstone inherits

In a longer capstone, a team has room to make more of those coordination decisions itself. CTS-285 does not add a Scrum Master role just to imitate that future environment; it hands the capstone a pod that already knows how to produce role-based evidence.

**Module 7 driving pressure:** can the system change underneath your feature and still preserve the behavior your tests say matters?
