## Three Roles, One Increment

Sprint 1 introduces a new way of working: you will contribute to one shared increment while keeping your own responsibilities, decisions, and evidence visible. The pod shares a product goal. Your grade does not.

Analyst rule

**A role tells you what you own at depth. It does not remove the core practices everyone must perform.**

### The three sprint roles

Product Owner

**Owns:** what the sprint commits to and what it refuses.

The Product Owner helps the pod stay aligned to product evidence, acceptance criteria, value, scope, and constraints. The role does not mean deciding by preference; sprint choices must remain traceable to evidence.

Developer

**Owns:** a defined slice of the sprint increment and the tests that cover that work.

The Developer implements behavior, traces it to the product evidence that justifies it, and leaves reviewable technical evidence through code, tests, commits, and pull-request activity.

Quality Lead

**Owns:** the standard the increment is held to.

The Quality Lead coordinates the Definition of Done and review evidence, checks whether the claimed result is supported, and helps the pod separate a real defect from a test problem, design difference, or unresolved source question.

### Everyone still does the core work

Specialization cannot become an excuse to avoid an outcome. Regardless of your Sprint 1 role, you will still:

- write or work with executable tests for your own contribution;
- review another implementation against evidence;
- contribute to the sprint increment;
- use acceptance criteria and source evidence when deciding whether behavior is correct;
- participate in the sprint review and retrospective process.

The role changes **what you own and are evaluated on at greater depth**, not whether you participate in the rest of the development process.

### What the course decides for the pod

In this first sprint, some process choices are already made for you. That is deliberate. You are learning to work inside a sprint before you are asked to design the process itself.

Course-set decision | Why it is set now

Sprint dates and cadence | The sprint needs a shared timebox before the pod can make meaningful scope decisions.

That planning, review, and retrospective occur | These practices create the evidence loop the module is teaching.

Definition of Done structure | You need a usable standard before learning how to refine one.

Git branch, pull-request, and review workflow | A common workflow keeps technical evidence comparable and reviewable.

Pod formation and initial role assignment | Sprint 1 is for learning the roles, not negotiating who gets which one.

Role rotation between M6 and M7 | You need experience from more than one perspective before the capstone handoff.

### Write the pod working agreement

Before Sprint 1 planning, make the pod's operating expectations visible. This is a shared working document, not a group grade. Record how the pod will communicate, coordinate work, keep work visible, respond when a commitment will not land, and raise blockers.

Your artifact

`docs/pod/working-agreement.md` — the pod's Sprint 1 operating agreement. Start from the supplied template and revise it when your retrospectives give you evidence to do so.

This file carries forward through M6 and M7. Module 8 will harden it into the capstone-facing agreement instead of asking the pod to invent a new process from memory.

### There is no Scrum Master role in CTS-285

That does not mean the process decisions normally associated with coordination disappear. In Sprint 1, the course makes those decisions explicitly so you can focus on learning the practices that produce evidence: testing, review, scope decisions, standards, and delivery.

In a longer capstone environment, teams have more room to own those coordination decisions themselves. CTS-285 prepares you for that handoff without pretending a four-week sprint gives every role enough time to be learned and evaluated well.

### A sprint role is not the same as a lab seat

Your Product Owner, Developer, or Quality Lead role lasts for the sprint. Some controlled activities later in the module may temporarily assign a different **seat** for one class activity so different students receive different information or perspectives.

Sprint role

Held across the module. Carries standing ownership. Produces individually attributable graded evidence.

Activity seat

Held only for one controlled activity. May change from activity to activity. Formative and not graded by seat.

### Shared product, individual evidence

The pod's increment is the context in which you work. Your grade comes from evidence that can be attributed to you: your contribution, the review you give, and the responsibilities you perform in your role.

How this is graded

A teammate's absence or unfinished work does not become your second grade. If pod capacity changes, sprint scope changes with it.
