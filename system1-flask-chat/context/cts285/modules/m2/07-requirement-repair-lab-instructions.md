## Requirement Repair Lab

Requirements often begin as useful but imperfect statements. In this lab, you will diagnose what makes a requirement weak and choose the repair that makes it clearer, more testable, and more defensible to a real development team.

Outcome

**MLO 2.3 → CLO 2 — Write Defensible Requirements:** Translate stakeholder and system evidence into clear, testable functional and non-functional requirements.

### Why requirements need repair

A requirement can sound reasonable and still create problems for a project team. Before a requirement becomes part of the DataMan Requirements Register, it should be clear enough to interpret consistently, supported well enough to defend, and specific enough that the team could later determine whether it was satisfied.

As you work through the lab, look for these common problems:

- **Vague:** Important words such as *fast*, *easy*, *simple*, or *modern* are not defined well enough to verify.
- **Bundled:** Several unrelated capabilities or qualities are packed into one requirement.
- **Solution-biased:** The statement prescribes an interface, database, technology, or other implementation before the evidence requires it.
- **Untestable:** The team cannot reasonably determine whether the requirement has been satisfied.
- **Unsupported:** The statement cannot yet be connected to stakeholder evidence, a demonstrated need, or a confirmed project constraint.

Analyst rule

**Your goal is not to make requirements longer.** A stronger requirement is clearer and more defensible, not more complicated.

### Use the five quality checks

Check | Ask yourself

**Clear** | Would another team member interpret this requirement the same way?

**Necessary** | Can I connect it to evidence, a user or stakeholder need, or a real project constraint?

**Testable** | Could the team later determine whether this requirement was satisfied?

**Solution-neutral enough** | Does it describe the needed outcome without locking the team into an unsupported implementation?

**Atomic enough** | Does it focus on one main capability or quality instead of combining unrelated ideas?

### How to work through the lab

1. Read the evidence, requirement, or stakeholder context carefully.
2. Identify the primary weakness in the current statement.
3. Choose the repair that best preserves the supported need.
4. Use the feedback to check your reasoning against the quality criteria.
5. Retry as needed. This is practice before you revise your own DataMan requirements.

### Connect the practice to DataMan

As you work, think about statements you have already encountered in the DataMan modernization project. After the lab, you will use the same quality checks to review and revise your own requirements before they become part of the final Requirements Register.

Caution

**Weak requirements create downstream problems.** The DataMan Requirements Register becomes input to Module 3 product work, so unclear or unsupported requirements produce weaker stories, acceptance criteria, and backlog decisions later.
