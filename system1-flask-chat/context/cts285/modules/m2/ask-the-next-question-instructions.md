# Ask the Next Question — Elicitation Practice

## Why you are doing this

Real stakeholders rarely give analysts complete, testable requirements. They describe frustrations, requests, preferences, and possible solutions. Your job is to decide what information is still missing and ask a useful next question without steering the stakeholder toward your own answer.

## What you will practice

In this activity, you will work through short stakeholder situations and decide:

- what question should come next;
- what evidence is still missing;
- when a stakeholder has jumped to a solution too early;
- how to move from a broad concern toward information that can support a requirement.

## How to approach each item

Before choosing an answer, ask yourself:

1. What do I know from the stakeholder statement?
2. What am I still assuming?
3. Which question would give me useful evidence without suggesting the answer?
4. Am I trying to understand the need before choosing a design or technology?

Use the feedback after each attempt. If you miss an item, review the explanation and try again.

## Why this matters for DataMan

After this practice, you will enter the DataMan elicitation case. There, the information will be less complete and your choices will affect what evidence you surface. The goal is not to guess a preferred solution. The goal is to gather enough evidence to justify what should—or should not—become a requirement.
