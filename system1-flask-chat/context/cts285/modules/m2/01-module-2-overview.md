## From Evidence to Requirements

Evidence

**Keep The DataMan Manual available throughout this module.** Find it in **Module 1 → Reference Resources → The DataMan Manual**. Treat it as primary product evidence when you classify, elicit, repair, and finalize requirements.

In Module 1, you learned to examine a system, notice ambiguity, and support conclusions with evidence. In Module 2, you will use that evidence to determine what a modern DataMan system must do, what qualities it must possess, and which questions still need answers.

### The project mission

Your team has been asked to modernize the 1977 Texas Instruments DataMan experience for a web-based environment. Before anyone designs screens or writes production code, the team must determine which behaviors, constraints, and learning goals belong in the modern system—and which ideas are still assumptions, preferences, proposed solutions, or unresolved questions.

Analyst rule

**Do not turn uncertainty into a requirement.** When the evidence is not strong enough, preserve the uncertainty as an open question or assumption and keep investigating.

### The analysis chain

**Evidence**

**Need**

**Requirement**

This module concentrates on that progression: use evidence to uncover and clarify the need before committing to a requirement.

### Your evidence base

You are not starting from a blank page. The original DataMan documentation gives you evidence about what the product was designed to do, how users interacted with it, and what constraints shaped its behavior. You will combine that evidence with stakeholder information, elicitation decisions, and new project conditions introduced throughout this module.

Evidence | Question to ask

Original DataMan behavior | What capability, rule, or learning behavior does this reveal?

Parent/teacher guidance | What user need or learning goal does this reveal?

Stakeholder statements | What is clear, what is ambiguous, and what must be clarified?

Simulation and elicitation decisions | What can the team defend now, and what remains uncertain?

Modern web context | Which original constraints still matter, and which need reinterpretation?

### How this module works

1. **Classify:** Learn how functional and non-functional requirements differ, then practice distinguishing requirements from vague statements and proposed solutions.
2. **Clarify:** Learn how analysts elicit information instead of accepting stakeholder requests at face value.
3. **Investigate:** Enter the Stakeholder Elicitation Under Pressure simulation, choose a limited set of questions, make an initial decision, and respond when new information changes the situation.
4. **Preserve evidence:** Save your simulation decision record in GitHub at `docs/decisions/m2-elicitation-decision-record.md`.
5. **Apply:** Launch the DataMan Elicitation Case from Canvas and use evidence-first reasoning to surface needs, uncertainty, and defensible project decisions.
6. **Repair:** Learn how to turn evidence into clearer, testable requirements, then use the controlled practice to repair statements that are vague, bundled, unsupported, untestable, or solution-biased.
7. **Review:** Use M2.5 — Requirements Quality Review to test and revise your own requirements while revision is still possible.
8. **Build:** Complete the DataMan Requirements Register in `docs/requirements.md` and submit its direct GitHub URL in Canvas.
9. **Carry forward:** Use the Module 2 Wrap-Up to confirm that your requirements are ready to become input to Module 3 product work.

Your artifact

`docs/decisions/m2-elicitation-decision-record.md` — preserves your simulation path, evidence, uncertainty, and revision decisions.

`docs/requirements.md` — your evaluated Module 2 Requirements Register containing evidence-backed functional and non-functional requirements.

How this is graded

Your primary evaluated artifact is the **DataMan Requirements Register**. Practice, simulation work, feedback, and quality review are designed to help you build and revise that artifact before submission.
