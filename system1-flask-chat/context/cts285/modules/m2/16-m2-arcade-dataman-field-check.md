## DataMan Field Check — Requirements Survey

A short, self-paced drill on classifying and writing DataMan requirements. Use it to find your weak spots before you finish your Requirements Register.

**This is optional and ungraded.** It does not appear in the gradebook, it produces no submitted evidence, and nothing here is turned in. Skip it if you already feel solid on requirements classification and elicitation, or use it as a quick check before you finalize your Requirements Register.

Your grade in Module 2 depends only on the **DataMan Requirements Register** and the work already listed in Canvas. This page changes none of that.

### What This Drills

The Field Check is a 21-item survey. Each run samples 20 items in random order and covers all three Module 2 outcomes:

Outcome | What it checks

**MLO 2.1 — Classify** | Sort DataMan manual statements into functional requirement, non-functional requirement, not-ready statement, or proposed solution.

**MLO 2.2 — Elicit and Clarify** | Choose the strongest elicitation move when evidence is thin, contradictory, or biased toward a solution.

**MLO 2.3 — Evidence to Requirement** | Turn a piece of DataMan evidence into a requirement that is testable and not tied to 1977 hardware or a specific technology.

Every prompt is drawn from the 1977 DataMan manual, including the places where the manual disagrees with itself. Every answer choice gets feedback that explains why it is strong, defensible, too early, or off target. After each run you get a per-outcome breakdown so you know which outcome to review.

### How It Works

- Pick one option per item. There is immediate feedback, not a silent right/wrong count.
- Some options are marked **near** — a defensible reading that is not scored against you, the same three-band relevance scale used elsewhere in this course.
- At the end of a run, you get a breakdown by MLO and a list of the items you missed, so you can go back and reread the right section before you revise your requirements.
- Progress is saved only in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere, nothing is collected, and nothing is visible to your instructor.
- Run it as many times as you want, or not at all.
