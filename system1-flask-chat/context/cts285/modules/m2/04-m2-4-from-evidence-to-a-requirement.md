## From Evidence to a Requirement

You have gathered evidence. Now the analyst has to convert that evidence into language a product team can understand, evaluate, and later turn into product work.

Outcome

**MLO 2.3** — Translate stakeholder and system evidence into clear, testable functional and non-functional requirements.

### A Good Requirement Has a Job

A requirement should help the team answer three questions:

- **What must be true?**
- **Why does it matter?**
- **How could we tell whether it has been satisfied?**

Caution

In this module, you are defining the requirement—not the user story, task, interface, database, or code. Those decisions come later.

### Use This Translation Pattern

Step | Question | Output

**1. Evidence** | What did the source, stakeholder, or observation actually show? | A factual statement you can support

**2. Need** | What problem, capability, or condition does that evidence reveal? | A concise need statement

**3. Requirement** | What must the system do or satisfy? | A clear, testable requirement

**4. Check** | Could a team later verify whether this requirement is met? | Revise if needed

### Quality Check

Before a requirement enters the register, it should be:

- **Clear** enough for another team member to interpret consistently.
- **Necessary** and traceable to evidence, a need, or a project constraint.
- **Testable** so the team can later determine whether it was satisfied.
- **Solution-neutral enough** to avoid prescribing implementation unless the implementation itself is constrained.
- **Atomic enough** to focus on one main capability or quality.

### The Hand-Off to Scrum

Requirements define what must be true about the system. In Module 3, the Product Owner and development team will translate approved requirements into user stories, acceptance criteria, estimates, and prioritized backlog work. A weak requirement creates weak product work downstream.
